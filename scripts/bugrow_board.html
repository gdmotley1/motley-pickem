The week scoreboard, second pass

The shift is a bug. Each player's row sizes its own columns to whatever is in that row. Nicole's 13 is a digit wider than everyone else's score, so her record lands 11.9px left of the other three. Every option below fixes it the same way, with fixed columns wide enough for 210, and that fix ships whichever one you pick.

The bar is a design problem, and a thicker bar would not fix it. It draws points banked against the most you can still finish on, out of 210, and that scale says almost nothing at either end of a week. At noon three of you can still finish on 210. By tonight all four of you are inside 36 points of each other. So every option is drawn twice, once at each end.

Real Week 2 numbers, run through the app's own scoring code. The noon card is what the database held at noon today; the night card plays the same twenty games out with four upsets.

Which bar I took you to mean

The thin strip along the bottom edge of each player's row: solid is banked, the pale run is the most they can still finish on. It is outlined in red on today's card. If you meant the grey sentence under the four rows instead, C makes that sentence readable and D replaces it.

What ships today

At noon the bar is four pale strips at 97–100%%, because nobody has spent anything yet, with a solid nub at 6%% under Nicole. At night the solid runs sit between 54%% and 71%% and the pale ends between 75%% and 80%%. There is no moment in the week where it says something.

The night card shows the record column going ragged a second way: Nicole's 9-7 is a character narrower than 12-4, so hers starts 5.3px further right.

What I would do

D, the race track

It is the only option whose picture gets more interesting as the week goes on, and the only one that answers the question you actually ask on a Saturday night, which is not “what fraction of 210 do I have” but “can I still catch her.” On the night card Parker is on 150, the lead line is drawn there, and all three other tails reach past it: the week is still open with four games left. A rail measured against 210 cannot show that at any thickness.

If you want it loud more than you want it to say something, E is the one. If you want no chance of misreading it, B, because a printed number has no scale to get wrong.

The one to avoid

A. It is the obvious answer to “you can hardly see it,” and it makes the graphic bigger without making it mean more. A six-pixel bar that says nothing is worse than a two-pixel one that says nothing, because now you look at it.
